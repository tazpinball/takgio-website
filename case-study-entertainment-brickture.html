Background

Brick building is having a moment with adults — as a hobby, a desk toy, and a gift. But turning a real object into a brick build you can actually put together is genuinely hard: working out the shape in three dimensions, choosing colors from a limited palette, keeping the model standing, and figuring out exactly which pieces to buy.

Brickture collapses that into one photo. Point your phone at a real-world object — a car, a mug, a fire hydrant — and get back a brick version of it: a rotatable 3D model that assembles itself on screen, step-by-step build instructions, and a parts list with estimated cost. It works with LEGO® bricks or any compatible system.

The Challenge

“Turn a photo into bricks” sounds like a filter. It isn't. A filter only has to look good on a screen; Brickture has to produce something that survives contact with the physical world.

  • One photo, three dimensions. A single picture shows one side of an object. The app has to infer the whole shape before it can build it.
  • A fixed, unforgiving palette. A photo holds millions of colors; a brick system offers a few dozen, and not every color exists in every piece.
  • Physics. A brick model has to stand up and hold together — overhangs, thin parts, and joints all need support.
  • Buildability. A design that can't be sourced or assembled is a picture, not a product. The output has to resolve into real parts and instructions a person can follow.

Our Approach

Brickture treats the conversion as a design problem with hard physical constraints. The pipeline reconstructs the object in 3D from the photo, rebuilds it in bricks at a buildable scale with colors drawn from the real brick palette, and designs the model to stand up on its own — then resolves the result into a parts list and step-by-step instructions.

The product is honest about what it's good at. It's built and marketed for chunky, iconic objects — vehicles, appliances, street objects, mugs — where a brick build reads instantly and the wow lands every time.

How It Works

  • Snap or import. Take a photo in the app or pull one from your camera roll, and crop to the object.
  • Watch it build. In about 90 seconds, the brick model appears and assembles itself on screen, brick by brick.
  • Explore it in 3D. Orbit the model and step through the build with a slider.
  • Get the parts list. Every piece by type and color, with an estimated cost.
  • Build it. Follow the step-by-step instructions from the first brick to the last.

Results

  • On both app stores in beta — TestFlight on iOS and Google Play on Android, with the first store builds shipped in August 2026.
  • 20 of 20 photos processed without a pipeline failure in stress testing, with 9 of 10 chunky objects rated clearly recognizable.
  • About 90 seconds from photo to finished model on the live cloud backend, with Sign in with Apple running in production.
  • Built with Claude end to end — 750+ commits since July 2026.

Status
