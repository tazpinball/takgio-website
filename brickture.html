Who it’s for

For adults who see brick-worthy objects everywhere

Brickture is marketed to adult builders, 21 and over. Great at chunky, characterful objects — cars, mugs, hydrants.

Desk-Toy Builder

“My actual car, in bricks, on my desk.”

Adult builders who want a model of their thing — their car, espresso machine or mailbox — not somebody else’s set.

Pain: Sets only cover what a toy company chose to design.
Gift Maker

“Dad’s old pickup as a build kit.”

People looking for a personal, one-of-a-kind gift for Father’s Day, birthdays and holidays.

Pain: Custom models usually mean designing it yourself, brick by brick.
Street Spotter

“That hydrant would make a great build.”

Builders who photograph interesting objects out in the world and want to turn them into a weekend project.

Pain: No practical way to go from a photo to a buildable parts list.
Benefits

The instructions and the kit are the product

Photo-to-bricks is the starting point. What sets Brickture apart is quality instructions and a way to get the parts.

Interactive 3D build guide

A step slider where the camera flies to where each piece goes, new bricks highlighted, per-step part callouts and step check-off.

Printable booklet

Written, illustrated instructions you can print and build from at the table.

Parts list with a cost estimate

Every part by type, color and size with real design IDs, plus an estimated cost — and LDraw export.

Kit ordering (in beta)

Pick a build and request its parts as a kit from inside the app.

Built to stand up

Automatic stability repair fixes floating pieces so the model holds together.

“Describe it”

No photo handy? Type a sentence and Brickture builds from the description.

How it works

From a photo to a buildable model in about two minutes

  1. Snap or describeTake a photo, pick one from your library, or type a description.
  2. Crop and pick a sizeXS (100 bricks) up to L (600) — bigger means more detail.
  3. Watch it buildIn about two minutes, the model assembles itself on screen, brick by brick.
  4. Follow the stepsInteractive 3D guide or printable booklet, with a parts list.
  5. Request the kitOrder the exact parts as a kit (in beta).

Do

  • Say “brick” or “brick-built”. If compatibility must be mentioned, say “compatible with leading brick brands”.
  • Show real builds. Label any box-art illustration as an illustration, “in testing”.
  • Say “in beta” for kit ordering.
  • Use American spelling (color, gray).

Don’t

  • Use any brick-brand name in ad copy, or name competitors.
  • Claim it works on anything — no people or pets, no “photorealistic”, no kids imagery.
  • Claim anyone has hand-built a model from the instructions.
  • Quote prices, credit packs or tiers — none are final.
  • Present box-art illustrations as the build output.
  • Say “no account” — sign-in is required.
  • Name the technology vendors behind the pipeline.
