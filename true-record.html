Who it’s for

For people who care what the documents actually say

People argue about Supreme Court decisions constantly and almost never read them. True Record lets them listen instead — on a commute, a walk, or at work.

Primary

Engaged listeners

Adults who already spend 5–15 hours a week with talk radio, news podcasts or audiobooks — while they drive, commute, walk or work with their hands.

Pain: The primary source is locked inside a PDF they will never open, so they rely on what a commentator said it said.
Professional

Journalists, staffers, law students, podcasters

People who want a fast audio pass over a report before deciding whether to read it properly.

Pain: Hours of reading just to find out whether a document matters.
Access

Blind and low-vision listeners

Official documents read aloud, with a read-along transcript and lock-screen and headphone controls.

Pain: Government PDFs are hard to use with a screen reader.
Education

Civics educators and law librarians

Primary sources students will actually listen to — majority, concurrences and dissents, each attributed.

Pain: Getting students to engage with the original text.
Benefits

The document, start to finish

Every narration is the official published text, read verbatim. Nothing is editorialized, summarized or paraphrased.

Read in their own words

Supreme Court opinions, House and Senate committee reports, and CRS reports, narrated from the official text.

Every view, attributed

Where a document has dissenting or minority views, they’re read and attributed — each its own chapter, with All, Majority and Dissents filters.

Read along

A transcript that highlights the sentence being spoken — free for every document.

An alert the morning it lands

Follow the Court, a committee or a topic for one plain, factual notification — never more than one a day.

Built for the commute

Offline downloads (Pro), speeds from 0.8× to 2×, skip back 30 and forward 15, lock-screen and headphone controls.

No ads, no tracking

No advertising, analytics or tracking. Sign in with Apple, Google or an emailed code — no passwords.

How it works

Open a document. Press play.

  1. Browse the catalogEach document shows its date, run-time and chapters.
  2. Listen freeA 15-second preview of anything, the Court’s syllabus in full, and the transcript.
  3. Go Pro for the whole thingFull narrations, dissents and concurrences, downloads.
  4. Follow what mattersGet a morning alert when a new document lands.

25-word description

Hear official government documents — Supreme Court opinions, committee reports, CRS reports — narrated verbatim. No panel, no commentary, no spin. The document, start to finish.

50-word description

True Record narrates official US government documents in their own words: Supreme Court opinions with every separate opinion attributed, congressional committee reports and Congressional Research Service reports. Listen on the commute with read-along transcripts, chapters, speed control and offline downloads. The Court’s syllabus and every transcript are free.

Key messages

  • Lead with the document, never the app.
  • Neutral by design: the official text, read verbatim, with every side’s views attributed.
  • Free way in: the syllabus plays free and every transcript is free to read.
  • Built for listening: chapters, speed, downloads, lock-screen controls.

  • Listeners: regular podcast, talk-radio and audiobook listeners; commuters.
  • Interests: the Supreme Court, Congress, law, civics, current affairs.
  • Professionals: journalists, legislative staff, law students, podcasters.
  • Access: blind and low-vision communities; civics educators and law librarians.

Do

  • Lead with the document, and name it neutrally.
  • Say “the documents in the catalog”.
  • Apply the neutrality test: would a reasonable person who disagrees say this misrepresented the document?
  • Include the independence disclaimer below in long-form placements.

Don’t

  • Say “every opinion” or “every report”.
  • Mention AI, summaries, CarPlay, Android, or tablets.
  • Frame anything as partisan, in either direction.
  • Use “official”, government seals, or anything built around “Congressional Record”.
  • Say it’s on the App Store until the listing is live.
  • Use screenshots that name individual politicians or show graphic case text.
