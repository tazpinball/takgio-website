A machine breaks. One scan tells the venue.

QR-code problem reporting for pinball and arcade venues.

Put a QR card on each machine. A player scans it, taps what’s wrong, and the venue gets an email with a one-tap Mark fixed link. No app. No account. When it’s fixed, the player who reported it can be told.

The problem

Pinball machines break quietly

Pinball machines are hard to diagnose by looking, which makes communicating a problem a big one for the industry.

It isn’t obvious

You often can’t tell a pop-up target is dead by looking at the machine. You find out by playing, which can take hours, or when someone who knows pinball tells you.

Most players can’t tell

Many players are casual. They may not realise a machine has a problem at all, and the ones who do have no standard way to say so.

News travels slowly

At the venue where we started, customers had no standard way to report a problem. Reports came in however they could, and it could take weeks or even months for the right person to find out.

Who it’s for

For venues with more machines than hours in the day

Built with a working pinball and arcade venue, for the people who run the floor and the people who play on it.

The owner

Venue owners and operators

Know which machines are down without walking the floor, and who is on it.

Pain: Hearing about a broken machine only when a customer mentions it.
The tech

Technicians and floor staff

Each report names the machine and the problem, so the fix starts with the right information.

Pain: Vague word of mouth: “one of the machines is acting up.”
The player

Players

Report it on the spot from a phone, then find out when it’s fixed.

Pain: Walking to the counter to explain, or giving up and moving on.
Benefits

The report reaches the right person, with the right details

Everything in Phase 1 is live today.

No app, no account

A player scans the card with their phone camera. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.

Six one-tap problems

Ball stuck, Flipper, Won’t start, Coin / credit, Display or Other, with an optional note and photo.

The venue is emailed

Every report emails the venue, with the machine and the problem in the subject line, like “[PA-01] Machine name: Coin / credit”.

One-tap Mark fixed

The email carries a Mark fixed link, with a 5-minute Undo for a mis-tap. A player who left their email is told it’s fixed.

Quiet when it should be

A problem that keeps being reported produces at most one “Still being reported” email a day.

A public status board

Anyone can see which of the venue’s machines are working, on a status page the venue shares.

How it works

Three steps from broken to fixed

  1. Scan the cardThe player points their phone camera at the QR card on the machine. No app and no account.
  2. Tap what’s wrongOne of six choices, plus an optional note, photo and email address.
  3. Mark it fixedThe venue gets an email and taps Mark fixed when the repair is done. A player who left their email is told.
Where it’s headed

Live today, with two more phases in progress

Phase 1 is in use now. Phases 2 and 3 are in progress and not yet released, and may change as the venue tells us what it needs.

Phase 1 Live beta

QR cards and the player report

  • QR cards on the machines
  • Player report: one tap, an optional note, photo and email
  • Email to the venue with a one-tap Mark fixed link
  • A public status board
Phase 2 In progress

Staff tools

  • A phone dashboard for owners, managers, techs and floor staff
  • Invite-only accounts: sign in by email link, password, Google or Apple
  • Steps like “I’ve got it”, “Need a part” and “Fixed”, and an out-of-order flag
  • Automatic escalation: a backup after 15 minutes, a manager after an hour
  • Phone notifications, and the venue’s logo inside the QR codes
Phase 3 In progress

Promotions

  • The venue’s own offers, like happy hour or events, shown after a report or on the status pages
  • Never inside the report form or in emails, and no outside ads
  • Optional make-up credit codes for players who lost credits, only if the venue wants them
Proof

In use, and fixing machines

57

problems reported by players

Since the beta went live on October 9, 2026, players have reported 57 problems. 54 are fixed, and the other 3 are waiting for parts to arrive. With the system in place, a typical repair takes about 2 hours.

Every email got through

84 report emails sent to the venue so far. None failed.

What gets reported most

Flipper problems, stuck balls and pop-up targets that don’t register a hit.

Developed over 6 weeks

6 weeks of development and discussion before the beta went live on October 9, 2026.

Privacy by design

The venue sees the problem, the note and any photo. It never sees a player’s email address.
